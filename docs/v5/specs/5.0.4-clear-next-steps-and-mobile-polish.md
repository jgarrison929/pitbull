## InEight comparison

The intel files describe a loop that InEight splits across products, with hand-offs between them. Facts below cite the intel. Lines marked **Inference** are ours.

- **Estimate to budget.** Control's budget comes from publishing InEight Estimate, an Excel import, or hand entry (estimate-to-budget C6). Units, account codes, and other fields must match between Estimate and Platform, or the publish fails (C9). The publish runs in the background, "could take several minutes", and errors can leave a partial import (C12). Re-publishing removes cost items, pay items, and change orders already in Control (C11). The intel's own inference: estimate to budget "is a project of its own in InEight" (I1).
- **Field plan to cost.** Approved quantities go to Plan quantity tracking, "which syncs with Control" (field-plan-and-progress C8). That sync is run by a user, and the documented cadence is "Weekly after Payroll cost is imported into Control" (C10). Hours reach Control only after payroll processes them, through a separate sync (C11). The intel's inference: same-minute posting after approval is a speed edge Pitbull can show (I2).
- **Change approval.** The documented change lifecycle runs issue, price, PCO, approve the PCO, CCO, internal approval, client eSignature, execute, "then approve the contract adjustment in Control" (change-orders C5). Approval workflows exist for budget moves, PCOs, CCOs, and documents (C8).

**Inference.** A user who works the whole InEight loop moves between Estimate, Progress, Change, and Control, and waits on syncs between them. Pitbull holds the loop in one database already. Its edge in 5.0.4 is one role-aware screen that lists the next step across the whole loop: estimate into budget, plan the day, report, approve, cost and earned value, and approve a change. The intel files do not say how InEight's home screens present next steps, so this spec makes no claim about them.

## User stories

1. As the superintendent, I sign in and the first thing I see is "Submit tomorrow's plan: Bridge Crew 2 on 25-031". One tap takes me to that plan with **Submit plan** on screen.
2. As the foreman on a phone, I sign in and see today's plan to report, not a chat log. One tap takes me to the report form with every quantity and hours field I must fill. Below it I see tomorrow's draft plan for my crew, which I can submit.
3. As the PM, I sign in and see "Approve CO-001 on 25-031: $60,000.00" and "Approve the estimate for Harbor Side Abutment (26-010)". One tap takes me to the change order with **Approve** on screen. Cost terms are spelled out the first time they appear.
4. As the executive, I sign in and see the portfolio's cost and earned value, with one plain sentence per at-risk job, such as "I-5 Culverts is over cost: it has spent $255K more than the work it has done is worth." One tap opens that job.
5. As any role on any page, I read a title and one line that says what the page is for.
6. As a first-time visitor, I can follow a short demo path through the loop, or dismiss it.
7. As anyone on a 320 px or 390 px phone, no page scrolls sideways, every number is readable, and each card leads with one number.

## 5.0.4-c: visual tokens and card primitives

The visual system extends the tokens already in `v5/src/app/globals.css`: one brand accent (`--brand`, safety amber), neutral slate, and three status tones (`--positive`, `--caution`, `--negative`, each with a `-soft` background). 5.0.3 Part C made every token work under `.dark`. 5.0.4 adds sizes and patterns. It adds no new color.

### Type scale

| Token | Size / line height | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `text-figure-primary` | 28 px / 32 px, semibold, `tabular-nums` | The one main number on a card |
| `text-title` | 20 px / 28 px, semibold | `h1` |
| `text-section` | 18 px / 24 px, semibold | `h2` section titles |
| `text-body` | 16 px / 24 px | Paragraphs, list rows, inputs, buttons |
| `text-label` | 14 px / 20 px, medium | Labels, secondary figures, meta lines |

No visible text is smaller than 14 px. Today `Kpi` labels use `text-xs` (12 px), and the phone tab bar uses `text-[11px]`. Both move to `text-label`.

### Spacing scale

Use the Tailwind 4 px step only at these values: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 px. Cards use 16 px padding on phones and 24 px at ≥ 1024 px. Stacked cards have 12 px gaps on phones and 16 px on desktop.

### Color and status

- `--brand` marks the one primary action on a screen (a filled button) and the active nav item. It never colors a number.
- Status tones color a number's delta and its badge only. They follow the one status rule in `v5/src/lib/status.ts`: index ≥ 1.00 is on track (`--positive`), 0.95 to under 1.00 is watch (`--caution`), under 0.95 is at risk (`--negative`), and a negative dollar variance is never green. A badge always carries a word ("On track", "Watch", "At risk"), so color is never the only signal.
- Hard-coded Tailwind colors such as `text-amber-700 dark:text-amber-400` in `cost-table.tsx` and `cost-items/[code]/page.tsx` move to the status tokens.

### Numbers

- Money uses `usdCents` for exact values and `usdShort` ($73.0M, $255K) for tile summaries. Each short figure carries its exact value in `title` and `data-cents`.
- Indexes show 2 decimals (0.93). Percents show whole numbers on cards (80%) and one decimal in tables (40.0%).
- Negative money uses the minus sign `−` (U+2212), not a hyphen.
- Every number rendered on a card goes through `Figure` in `format.tsx`, which sets `data-figure="primary"` or `data-figure="secondary"`.

### Card pattern

New `MetricCard` in `components/app/metric-card.tsx` replaces `Kpi`:

- one `label` (`text-label`), one primary `Figure`, and at most 2 secondary figures in one `sub` line
- an optional `details` region, rendered as a native `<details>` with a `<summary>` of at least 44 px height, that holds the rest
- `data-card` on the root, so Verify can count per card

A list row on phones uses the same shape: name, one primary figure, up to 2 secondary figures, and a `<details>` for the rest.

### Tap targets

Every `a`, `button`, `input`, `select`, `textarea`, and `summary` that is visible outside running text has a hit box of at least 44×44 px at widths ≤ 640 px. Inputs use 16 px text, so iOS Safari does not zoom on focus.
