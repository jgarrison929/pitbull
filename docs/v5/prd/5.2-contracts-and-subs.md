# PRD: 5.2 Contracts and subs

Status: written 2026-10-26 (PT) against `main` at `d56eb6be`. Sources: [contracts intel](../intel/contracts.md) (#694 on the old history), [change orders intel](../intel/change-orders.md), [cost and earned value intel](../intel/cost-and-earned-value.md), and the [fresh-start plan](../fresh-start-plan.md) sections 3, 4, and 9. The [roadmap](../../roadmap.md) holds the dates. Increment specs: [5.2.0](../specs/5.2.0-subcontracts-and-commitments.md), [5.2.1](../specs/5.2.1-committed-cost-forecast.md). Specs for 5.2.2 to 5.2.4 come next.

## Decisions

- **One subcontract record.** A subcontract has a vendor, one retention rate, and schedule-of-values (SOV) lines. Each line maps to one leaf cost item. No bid packages, master agreements, or purchase orders (recommendation 1, recommendation 8).
- **Executing commits cost.** A draft commits nothing. Commitment per cost item is the sum of its executed SOV lines, plus executed sub change orders from 5.2.3 (M1, without tax).
- **Committed Cost is a third forecast method.** EAC = AC + open commitment (M3). With no other cost on the item, that equals the executed commitment. Uncommitted Current Budget and pending sub changes show next to EAC and never enter it (recommendation 3, I1).
- **Pay applications are in 5.2.** Without them, open commitment never drops and AC cannot reconcile with commitments (I2, recommendation 2). One pay application record with states replaces InEight's invoices, claims, and payment forms (I6).
- **Strict defaults, not settings.** Billing past 100% of a line is refused. A retention release above retention held is refused. A commitment above Current Budget needs the executive. A sub change order whose lines do not add up cannot execute (recommendation 4, I4).
- **Validate before approval.** Every check runs on submit. An approved pay application is final. Corrections go on the next one (recommendation 7, I7).
- **Out until Joshua decides:** the insurance-expiry hold on pay applications (recommendation 5). See [Open question](#open-question-for-joshua).

## Problem

On `main`, a cost item splits Original Budget, Current Budget, and Current Estimate into labor, material, equipment, and sub cents (`ob_sub_cents`, `cb_sub_cents`, `ce_sub_cents` in `v5/src/db/schema.ts`). Change order lines carry a `sub_cents` amount. There is no vendor, subcontract, commitment, or pay application table. Subcontract cost reaches the seed only as cost entries with notes such as "Mass excavate subcontract" on 24-118 / 03-200 (`v5/src/seed/index.ts`).

So a PM cannot answer "what have we committed to subs on this job, and what is still open?" The forecast methods are Current Budget and Average Performance (`forecast_method` enum). Neither reads a commitment, so a job that has bought out its sub scope still forecasts from budget.

InEight Contract runs "from contract creation through pay request approval" and pushes commitment amounts to project forecasts (C1). Its Committed Cost forecast method sets forecast final cost to total committed cost (M3). The fresh-start plan maps InEight Contract to 5.2 (plan section 3).

## Users and roles

5.2 adds no role. The PM owns subcontracts, the way the Estimator and the scheduler were folded into the PM.

| Role | Owns in 5.2 | Reads |
|---|---|---|
| Project Manager | Create, edit, and execute subcontracts. Enter and submit sub pay applications. Draft sub change orders. Request retention release | Commitments, open commitment, retention held, uncommitted CB, pending sub exposure |
| Executive | Execute a commitment that exceeds Current Budget. Approve or reject sub pay applications, sub change orders, and retention releases | The same, across the portfolio |
| Superintendent | Nothing new | The subcontracts on his jobs, read-only (scope and vendor, so he knows who is on site) |
| Foreman | Nothing new | Nothing. No subcontract nav entry |

InEight's main users are contract managers and contract administrators, with field handlers, PMs, and controllers reading pay forms (C3). Mid-size heavy-civil contractors often have the PM run subcontracts. Folding the contract manager into the PM keeps four roles. The executive approves money leaving the company, which is the separation of duties a controller expects.

## InEight comparison

What we copy, with claim IDs:

- A contract record with line items mapped to a WBS code (C6) and an SOV that splits each line into quantity, unit, unit price, and value (C11).
- Retention on the header, pre-filling each line (C7, C37). Retention release capped at retention withheld (C38).
- Executed contracts drive committed cost in Control (C18). The Committed Cost forecast method (M3).
- SOV progress math: value claimed = quantity × unit price, percent = value ÷ schedule value (M6). Payment math: to-date gross, net payable = current gross − retention (M4). Retained to date (M5).
- Vendor change orders logged per contract with a total, linked to the owner-side change (C22, C26). A change must be fully allocated before it executes (C24). An executed one cannot be reopened (C27).
- A certified claim is locked and snapshotted (C31).

What we refuse to copy:

- Eight contract statuses (C5). Pitbull uses `draft`, `executed`, `voided`, and from 5.2.4 `closed`.
- Five payment records: invoices, payment claims, payment forms, accruals, goods receipts (C21, C30 to C34, I6). Pitbull has one pay application.
- Warn-or-block settings per contract type (C14, C20, C35, I4). Pitbull picks the strict default and documents it.
- ERP publish and the proxy ERP (C4, C34, C49). Pitbull has no accounting integration before 5.6. Retention lives in the app, because InEight leaves payment and release status to the ERP or to hand entry (C40, I3, R2).
- Validation that fails after submit (R3). Pitbull validates before approval (I7).

What we do not claim: we do not know whether InEight blocks payment on an expired insurance certificate (U1), how its committed forecast treats an approved but unexecuted vendor change (U2), or whether retention can step down by rule (U3). This PRD makes no claim about those.

## Scope

### In scope for 5.2

1. Vendors (a name per company) and subcontracts on an approved job, numbered SC-001, SC-002, and so on per job.
2. SOV lines with description, unit, quantity, unit price, and one leaf cost item. Amount = quantity × unit price, rounded half-up to the cent, in integer math.
3. Execute: draft → executed. Executed is final. A commitment that would push a cost item over its Current Budget needs the executive.
4. Commitment, uncommitted Current Budget, and open commitment per cost item, per job, and on the portfolio.
5. The Committed Cost forecast method per leaf cost item (EAC = AC + open commitment).
6. Sub pay applications: `draft` → `submitted` → `approved`, or `rejected`. Quantity to date per SOV line. Gross, retention, and net payable per M4 and M5. Approval posts current gross to AC once and lowers open commitment.
7. Sub change orders that add, change, or deduct SOV quantity or value, with an optional link to an owner change order line. Pending sub exposure next to EAC while a sub change order is not executed.
8. Retention release, capped at retention held. Subcontract close-out when nothing is open.
9. Typed actions for every write, so the assistant rail runs the same actions as the screens.
10. An audit row for every state change on subcontracts, sub change orders, pay applications, and releases (intel acceptance check 15).

### Out of scope for 5.2

- Bid packages, master agreements, Contract Writer, DocuSign, purchase orders, goods receipts, accruals, taxes, multiple currencies, a vendor portal, and accounting integration (recommendation 8).
- Insurance, surety, and other supporting documents, and any hold on payment for an expired certificate. That is the [open question](#open-question-for-joshua). Forms and inspections stay in 5.4.
- Lien waivers (U4). Retention that steps down by rule (U3). Per-line retention overrides and waivers (C37). One rate per subcontract in 5.2.
- Back charges (C33), pay-when-paid (C41), and overpayment allowances (C35).
- Subs claiming quantities from daily plans (C17). Sub pay applications take quantities the PM enters from the sub's billing.
- Owner billing and AIA G702 and G703. That is 5.5.
- Export of approved pay applications to accounting. That is 5.6.

## Forecast rules (locked)

- **Commitment** on a cost item = Σ amount of SOV lines on executed subcontracts + Σ executed sub change order lines (from 5.2.3). Drafts and voided subcontracts count $0.00.
- **Billed to date** on a cost item = Σ current gross of approved pay application lines on it (from 5.2.2).
- **Open commitment** = commitment − billed to date. It never goes below $0.00, because billing past a line's value is refused.
- **Committed Cost EAC** = locked AC + open commitment. ETC = open commitment. AC is the same locked AC the other methods read (the one cutoff from milestone M4 of the fresh-start plan).
- When all AC on the item comes from its subcontract, EAC equals the executed commitment (M3). When the item also has self-performed cost, EAC adds it once.
- **Uncommitted CB** = Current Budget − commitment. It shows next to EAC. Negative means "over Current Budget by".
- **Pending sub exposure** (from 5.2.3) = Σ sub change order lines in `draft` or `submitted`. It shows next to EAC and never enters it (I1).
- An approved owner change order on a Committed Cost item raises Current Budget and leaves EAC alone. The job page lists that change as not yet covered by a commitment (recommendation 6, intel acceptance check 10).
- Current Budget and Average Performance items behave as on `main`. Executing a subcontract on them changes no forecast.

## Data model sketch

Against `v5/src/db/schema.ts` at `d56eb6be`. Money is integer cents (`money()`), quantities are integer thousandths (`milli()`), and every row has `company_id` (`companyRef()`). Only one server module writes each group of tables, through `run()`.

```ts

  id: uuid("id").primaryKey(),

  name: text("name").notNull(),          // unique per company, case-insensitive

  id: uuid("id").primaryKey(),
  companyId: companyRef(),

  vendorId: uuid("vendor_id").notNull().references(() => vendors.id),
  number: integer("number").notNull(),   // shown as SC-001; unique per job
  title: text("title").notNull(),

  retentionBp: integer("retention_bp").notNull(),  // basis points: 1000 = 10.00%
```

## Trust-bar risks

- **Empty states (item 1).** The crawl fails on an empty state over seeded data, so every active job seeds at least one subcontract. Harbor (draft estimate) has no Subcontracts entry until its estimate is approved.
- **Nothing announced early (items 1 and 4).** No tab, button, or label for pay applications, sub change orders, or retention release appears before its increment ships. 5.2.0 shows "Retention 10%" as a term of the subcontract and no "retention held" amount.
- **Two EAC writers (item 6).** EAC still comes only from `itemForecast` and `eacSql`. Change order approval computes its EAC delta through the same method-aware code, so a committed item records an EAC delta of $0.00.
- **Double-counted cost (item 3).** Sub cost entered as a loose cost entry and again as a pay application would count twice. 5.2.2 converts the seeded invoice rows. A seed test fails if a seeded cost item has both.
- **Exact numbers (item 3).** Every money check is to the cent in integer math. SQL and JS agree on each fixture.
- **Approved is final (item 6).** Executed subcontracts, approved pay applications, and executed sub change orders refuse edits on screen and through the assistant. A double approve posts once (unique index on the posting row).
- **Speed (item 1).** Subcontract and cost item pages stay under 1.5 s on staging. Commitment reads are one grouped query per job page.
- **Reset (item 2).** Reset demo restores every subcontract, line, and state to the seed. The reset median stays at or under 1,500 ms.
- **Words (item 1).** Labels say "Commitment", "Open commitment", "Uncommitted Current Budget", "Retention". No screen calls Pitbull an accounting system.

## Open question for Joshua

**Should approving a sub pay application be refused when the sub's general liability certificate has expired?** (Contracts intel recommendation 5, inference I5, acceptance check 12.)

What it needs: an insurance expiry date per subcontract (or per vendor), a refusal that names the certificate and date, and a way to record a renewed date. It is about one cloud-agent PR on top of 5.2.2.

What we know: InEight stores insurance documents with expiry dates and sends notices (C42, C43), and placeholders can block publishing (C44). We do not know whether it blocks payment (U1), so we must not claim it does not.

Recommendation: yes, as 5.2.5 on 2026-12-03, right after 5.2.4. It is small, it turns a real risk (paying a sub with no coverage) into a hard stop, and it fits "strict defaults". It stays out of 5.2.0 to 5.2.4 until you decide. Documents, surety, and lien waivers stay out either way.
